# Concise Report Writing

## Overview

Use this skill to produce plain, focused prose. Apply it to reports, briefs, docs, PR text, commit messages, product copy, and internal notes.

Do not apply these rules to code, identifiers, commands, quoted text, or technical terms where a simpler substitute would reduce precision.

## Workflow

1. Identify the audience, purpose, and required format from the request or surrounding project.
2. Lead with the main point.
3. Use concrete nouns and strong verbs.
4. Keep each paragraph focused on one idea.
5. Remove filler, repetition, vague claims, and inflated language.
6. Preserve technical accuracy and project terminology.
7. Review the final prose against the rules below before delivering it.

## Rules

- Avoid familiar metaphors, similes, cliches, and stock phrases.
- Use a short word when it means the same thing as a long word.
- Remove every word that does not add meaning.
- Prefer active voice when it makes the sentence clearer.
- Replace jargon, foreign phrases, and unnecessary technical language with plain English when precision survives.
- Keep established technical terms when replacements would be less precise.
- Match the tone and terminology of the project.
- Break any rule when following it would make the writing unclear, awkward, misleading, or incorrect.

## Editing Checklist

- Put the answer, finding, or recommendation first.
- Delete throat-clearing such as "it is important to note" unless it adds needed caution.
- Replace vague modifiers with facts or remove them.
- Split long sentences when they carry more than one idea.
- Prefer direct verbs over noun phrases.
- Keep bullets parallel and useful.
- Avoid repeating the same claim in the heading, intro, and bullets.
- Keep caveats near the claim they qualify.

## Precedence

A project-level `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, style guide, or user instruction can add or override these rules. When rules conflict, follow the more specific instruction.
